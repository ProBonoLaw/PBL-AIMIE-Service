# Materiality Review Examples

Use these as decision patterns, not as case facts or a fixed event-type hierarchy.

## Hearing and order lifecycle

Requested scope: `major procedural events`.

- Retain a motion that initiates a material custody dispute.
- Exclude a routine hearing-scheduling notice unless it changes the proceeding or explains a material delay.
- Retain the hearing when testimony, argument, or a ruling materially advances the issue.
- Retain temporary and final orders separately when each changes the operative state.

Do not omit the final order merely because an earlier arbitration decision or interim order appears to resolve the same subject.

## Discovery

Requested scope: `major events, including discovery`.

- Retain a deposition that supplies material testimony.
- Retain a refusal to execute releases when it creates a material discovery dispute.
- Exclude ordinary emails selecting the deposition time unless scheduling itself becomes disputed or consequential.

For a complete discovery history, the scheduling, service, production, objection, motion, and disposition may each meet the lower scope-specific threshold.

## Parenting time

Requested scope: `major events and incidents`.

- Ordinarily exclude a routine supervised visit that occurred as planned.
- Retain a denied, missed, interrupted, or materially changed visit when it evidences noncompliance, conflict, escalation, or a change in restrictions.
- Retain an order changing supervision, duration, or decision-making conditions.

For a parenting-time-compliance timeline, ordinary completed visits may be material because the request makes compliance history itself the subject.

## Repeated emergency encounters

The evidence independently supports four emergency-department visits on four dates. If the requested scope includes emergencies and each encounter materially documents the safety or medical course, retain four atomic events. Do not replace them with one week-long event.

If the evidence only states that `four emergency visits occurred during the week`, do not invent dates or four entries. Route the aggregate reference to gap analysis. If the individual encounters remain unresolved, a dated evaluation that documented the four visits may be retained as an atomic evaluation or reporting event when material; the underlying plural visits are not one atomic occurrence.

## CPS or child-welfare sequence

When supported and within scope, treat these as independently reviewable:

1. A report or referral was made.
2. The agency opened or conducted an investigation.
3. The agency made a finding or disposition.
4. A court, evaluator, or party later acted on that disposition.

Retain the material stages; exclude routine administrative follow-up that adds no significant action or state change. Preserve allegations and agency findings with their actual evidentiary character.

## Minor financial dispute

An arbitral ruling awarding a small reimbursement may be material to a comprehensive procedural history because it resolves a submitted issue. It may be excluded from a timeline limited to major custody, relocation, safety, and child-welfare events unless it explains another retained ruling or the user expressly requests all arbitration outcomes.

The amount alone does not decide materiality; the requested scope and procedural significance do.

## Allegation and denial

A serious allegation and a later denial may both be material even when neither is adjudicated. Retain them as separate attributed events when they frame a material dispute or lead to a later investigation, restriction, or ruling. Do not treat retention as a credibility determination.

## Routine communication with consequential effect

An ordinary-looking email may be material when the evidence shows that it gave required notice, rejected a proposed action, changed a party's position, triggered a response, or documented noncompliance. Retain the communication for its supported procedural or evidentiary function, not because email is inherently material.

## Issue-lifecycle check

The retained set contains a relocation request, interim relocation ruling, and arbitration decision, while the evidence references later court implementation. Treat the missing final relocation disposition as a gap or completeness concern. Do not assume the arbitration decision was the final court order and do not stop merely because the timeline already has a plausible ending.
