# Criminal-Case Materiality

Use this reference to apply timeline materiality review to criminal investigations and prosecutions. Treat it as a jurisdiction-neutral decision guide, not as a statement of substantive criminal law.

## Governing boundary

- Preserve the exact offense, charge, defense, and procedural terminology supplied by the records. Do not normalize `battery`, `assault`, `breaking and entering`, `burglary`, `fraud`, or another label into a supposedly equivalent offense.
- Do not supply statutory elements from memory. Elements, available defenses, charging procedures, and offense names differ by jurisdiction and may change with the charged subsection or case posture.
- Treat a source's reference to an element, intent, knowledge, permission, ownership, identity, causation, conspiracy, or loss as an assertion unless a returned ruling or verdict establishes the characterization.
- Retain inculpatory, exculpatory, impeachment, and mitigating events under the same request-relative standard.
- Do not equate arrest, indictment, information, complaint, charge, plea, verdict, or sentence. Preserve each supported act and its distinct procedural function.
- Do not infer guilt from charging, probable-cause findings, detention, asset restraint, or conviction of another participant.

## Scope modes

Identify the requested mode before selecting events:

- **Incident timeline:** Focus on the alleged conduct, immediate precursors and aftermath, reporting, injury or loss, and contemporaneous evidence.
- **Investigation timeline:** Include reports, interviews, surveillance, warrants, searches, seizures, forensic work, evidence transfers when material, investigative findings, referrals, and charging decisions.
- **Procedural timeline:** Focus on charging, appearances, release or detention, discovery, motions, pleas, trial, verdict, sentencing, post-trial proceedings, and appeal.
- **Complete case timeline:** Retain material events across incident, investigation, prosecution, disposition, and post-judgment stages.
- **Transaction or scheme timeline:** Preserve the individual representations, approvals, transactions, transfers, records, benefits, concealment acts, alerts, and investigative responses within the stated scheme or period.

Honor narrower limits on a person, count, victim, transaction, property item, account, location, date range, investigative agency, or procedural issue.

## Material issue map

Track only issues supported or referenced by the returned records. Depending on scope, material issues may include:

- identity or identification;
- act, attempt, omission, threat, entry, taking, possession, transfer, or representation;
- time, place, jurisdiction, venue, permission, authority, ownership, custody, or control;
- injury, fear, damage, loss, benefit, restitution, or victim impact;
- intent, knowledge, mistake, accident, authorization, self-defense, defense of others, alibi, duress, or another source-raised theory;
- witness perception, prior statement, recantation, correction, contradiction, bias allegation, or impeachment;
- physical, medical, digital, financial, documentary, surveillance, location, or forensic evidence;
- search, seizure, consent, warrant, interrogation, identification procedure, evidence preservation, or disclosure;
- charging, joinder, severance, competency, release, detention, plea, trial, sentencing, restitution, forfeiture, or appeal.

An event is not material merely because it can be assigned to one of these topics. Retain it only when it also satisfies the main skill's scope, significance, incremental-value, and evidence-discipline tests.

## Criminal-case lifecycle

Use this conceptual lifecycle to identify coverage gaps:

`alleged conduct → report or referral → investigation → evidence collection or testing → charging decision → charge → initial appearance or arraignment → release or detention decision → discovery → pretrial motions → plea or trial → verdict or dismissal → sentencing, restitution, or forfeiture → appeal or post-judgment proceeding`

Not every case uses every stage, and stages may occur in a different order. State and federal procedures differ. Do not create an event or require a stage merely because it ordinarily exists.

When the evidence shows that a material matter remained pending, was reserved, was remanded, required later implementation, or produced a later proceeding, search for the supported disposition before declaring closure. Keep these distinct when supported:

- complaint, citation, information, indictment, superseding charge, amendment, and dismissal;
- arrest, booking, initial appearance, arraignment, and detention or release ruling;
- discovery request, disclosure, objection, motion, hearing, ruling, production, and sanction;
- warrant application, issuance, execution, return, inventory, forensic examination, and suppression ruling;
- plea offer, acceptance or rejection, plea entry, plea withdrawal, verdict, and judgment;
- conviction, presentence report, sentencing submissions, sentencing hearing, sentence, restitution order, forfeiture order, and amended judgment;
- notice of appeal, appellate briefing, argument, decision, mandate, remand, and post-remand disposition.

## Offense-pattern guidance

### Assaultive conduct

Consider separately supported acts or states involving alleged threats, physical contact, use or display of a weapon, injuries, medical evaluation or treatment, emergency calls, contemporaneous observations, witness accounts, identification, claimed permission or justification, police response, protective restrictions, arrest, and charging or adjudication.

Do not collapse alleged contact, resulting injury, later treatment, reporting, and arrest into one `assault incident`. Do not state that an injury was caused by a person merely because the injury was documented after the alleged encounter unless the returned evidence supports that relationship.

### Unlawful entry and property offenses

Consider alleged approach, entry or attempted entry, access method, force or damage, permission or lack of permission, occupancy, alarm or surveillance activation, conduct inside the location, taking or attempted taking, departure, property discovery, possession, recovery, identification, search, arrest, charge, and disposition.

Keep entry, property damage, taking, possession, and recovery separate. Do not infer intent at entry, ownership, lack of authorization, or identity from proximity or later possession alone.

### Financial and white-collar matters

Treat `scheme` as an organizing issue, not as one timeline event. When individually supported and material, preserve:

- representations, omissions, certifications, promises, or instructions;
- approvals, overrides, access grants, account openings, or control changes;
- invoices, orders, trades, journal entries, transfers, deposits, withdrawals, conversions, or disbursements;
- receipt, custody, use, diversion, concealment, return, freezing, seizure, or recovery of funds or assets;
- compliance alerts, internal reports, audits, holds, escalations, remediation, discipline, or regulatory referrals;
- subpoenas, warrants, productions, interviews, cooperation acts, charging decisions, pleas, trial findings, loss determinations, restitution, and forfeiture.

Do not merge a repeated transaction series into one range when the individual transactions are supported and material. Do not describe parallel transactions as a common plan, conspiracy, concealment, laundering, fraud, or benefit to a participant unless the evidence supports that characterization.

## Evidence and relationship guardrails

- Distinguish the alleged offense from the later report, interview, testimony, expert opinion, charge, finding, plea, verdict, and sentence.
- Retain a material denial, alibi account, recantation, correction, alternative explanation, or exculpatory test result with accurate attribution.
- Preserve collection, transfer, storage, testing, result, disclosure, challenge, and admissibility ruling as separate evidence-lifecycle events when independently material.
- Do not label evidence as authenticated, contaminated, planted, exculpatory, inculpatory, or part of an unbroken chain of custody unless the returned source supplies that characterization.
- Treat a laboratory result, medical opinion, financial analysis, or expert conclusion as the source's finding, not as independently established fact.
- Treat surveillance, device, account, IP-address, location, or transaction attribution as a supported association only to the extent the records establish the person-to-device, person-to-account, or person-to-act link.
- Express motive, intent, knowledge, agreement, participation, causation, and consciousness of guilt only when source-supported and with the source's evidentiary character.

## Repetition and multi-actor cases

- Keep each materially distinct alleged act, victim, entry, transaction, communication, payment, evidence item, search, interview, charge, count, ruling, and disposition atomic.
- Do not create one event per count when several counts arise from the same atomic occurrence; counts and events are different analytical units.
- Do not merge separate occurrences merely because a source calls them a `pattern`, `series`, `course of conduct`, or `scheme`.
- Do not attribute one person's act, statement, possession, knowledge, or intent to another participant without direct relationship evidence.
- Treat agreement, direction, authorization, aiding, concealment, and proceeds-sharing as separate supported relationships or acts rather than consequences of association.
- When a source reports `dozens of transactions`, `multiple assaults`, or `several break-ins` without resolving the underlying occurrences, create an aggregate-occurrence gap. Do not invent individual events or compress the plural acts into one occurrence.

## Exclusion guidance

For a major-events criminal timeline, ordinarily exclude routine transport, housing assignments, standard scheduling, unchanged continuances, duplicate evidence descriptions, ministerial docket notices, routine discovery logistics, and repetitive status communications.

Retain an otherwise routine act when it changes custody status, access to evidence, trial timing, charge exposure, conditions of release, witness availability, preservation of evidence, a party's position, or the disposition of a material issue. A continuance, transport, or administrative failure may become material when it causes a consequential delay, missed proceeding, evidence loss, release, sanction, or other supported state change.

## Final audit

Before handing the retained set to completeness review:

1. Confirm the selected events match the requested scope mode and every expressly named category.
2. Confirm the alleged conduct, investigation, charging, adjudication, and disposition are neither conflated nor assumed.
3. Confirm significant prosecution and defense evidence received the same materiality standard.
4. Confirm separately supported injuries, entries, transactions, searches, interviews, rulings, and dispositions remain atomic.
5. Confirm no statement converts temporal proximity, association, ownership, access, employment, or account control into identity, intent, agreement, causation, or guilt.
6. Confirm every material pending issue referenced by the evidence was either resolved by a supported event or routed to gap analysis.
7. Confirm excluded routine events would not alter the reader's understanding of a charge, defense, evidentiary dispute, custody status, remedy, or outcome.
